I've taught online for nearly my entire career. I'm an ardent defender of asynchronous online education, because I know that it can be (and often is) done really well.

Yet, Ryan is right here that universities have worrying incentives to collect tuition and not look too hard at whether learning is happening. I see it happening in face-to-face settings at my own insitution, and asynchronous online education does create more potential for things to go wrong. A university that puts pressure on a health oriented program to not worry so much about vaccine compliance so that students can register for courses and pay tuition is also going to be tempted to encourage cutting corners in online classes.
