I think one of the most helpful concepts from Ellul's *Humiliation of the Word* is the idea of the artifice of the image. It's not that the image doesn't have the qualities that we attribute to it, it's that it doesn't have those qualities perfectly.

As a result, it's the gap between the promise of a medium or technology and the reality of that medium or technology that becomes dangerous. We accept the promise undercritically, and that's where we get into trouble.

I want to spend more time with this Ellulian artifice, because I think it's really helpful, including in cases like this one. We believe in the promises that edtech (and this is edtecy) gives us, and we don't fully recognize the artifice. This company claims that "missing a real gun is the worst possible outcome," but I'm not sure that's the case.
